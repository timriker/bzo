bzo test modes

Each link starts the game with one knob set. They measure, they do not configure: nothing here is saved, and a plain reload of / puts it back. Press Back to return to this list.

Start normally

The baseline every other mode is read against. Take this sample first.

Play/

shadows

Off drops the whole projected shadow pass: the caster stencil draws, the darkening overlay over the ground, and the per-frame projection. The matrix walk the pass needs is not dropped with it, because every mode pays that one and a phase has to mean the same thing in all of them.

Shadows ondefault Shadows off?shadows=0

celestial

Off drops the sun and moon discs. Three draws, but large ones: they are sized by the angle they subtend and sit at twice the world away.

Sun and moon ondefault Sun and moon off?celestial=0

antialias

Off asks for a context without MSAA, which an immersive session inherits for its own framebuffer. Chrome carries a driver workaround declaring MSAA unacceptable on Intel GPUs, and those are the machines that read slowest, so its cost has to be measured rather than assumed.

MSAA ondefault MSAA off?antialias=0

renderScale

Draw into a buffer this fraction of the window on each axis and let the browser present it across the whole window. It separates the pixels bzo draws from the surface the browser shows them on, which resizing a window cannot, because that moves both at once. Clamped to 0.25. It does nothing inside an XR session, where the framebuffer belongs to the headset rather than to the page.

Fulldefault Three quarters?renderScale=0.75 Half?renderScale=0.5 Quarter?renderScale=0.25

xrScale

renderScale for a session, and a knob of its own because renderScale cannot reach one: the framebuffer belongs to the headset rather than to the page. This is a fraction of the resolution the runtime recommends, so 1 is native and 0.7 is about half the pixels. It is the only way to ask a headset for fewer of them, and it does nothing outside a session. The drawing buffer in the sample says whether it took: full native on a Quest 2 reads 2880x1584.

Nativedefault Four fifths?xrScale=0.8 Seven tenths?xrScale=0.7 Half?xrScale=0.5

xrRate

The cadence to ask the runtime for. A Quest 2 offers 60, 72, 80, 90 and 120, and asked for nothing it reports back frameRate=0 -- it has chosen one and will not say which. Whatever it chose is the rate its compositor runs at, and a compositor at 90 or 120 reprojects a client delivering 30 three or four times over, on the same chip. bzo now asks for 72, the headset's own rate; these say what the others do. The [WebXR] line at session start reports what was asked for and what came back.

72default 60?xrRate=60 90?xrRate=90 120?xrRate=120

Together

Knobs combine. The first two are the fill question in a headset, which is where the evidence points: MSAA off, fewer pixels, and both at once. The third bounds what the whole scene beyond the tanks and the world is worth.

No MSAA, 0.7 scale?antialias=0&xrScale=0.7 0.7 scale alone?xrScale=0.7 Extras off?shadows=0&celestial=0&antialias=0

Global login probe

Not a knob and not a feature — a one-off probe of bzflag.org's global registration. It sends you to my.bzflag.org to log in with your global callsign, comes back with a token, and asks the list server whether that token is real. bzo never sees your password; the login form is bzflag.org's own, which is what the check requires. The answer is printed as plain text and also lands in the server log as [LOGIN] lines. Nothing is stored and nothing is granted.

It also reports whether you are in any of the groups adminGroups in server.json names, which is the rule a real login here would use. The list server answers about only the groups it was asked about, so that list is the whole question — and it comes from the config, never from the URL, because a group a client could name is a permission a client could name. Knowing the answer still grants nothing today.

Log in with a bzflag.org callsign/login

Reading the result: a sample lands in the server log as renderer.stats a while into every map and again when the debug HUD closes, so a headset that never opens the HUD still reports one. draw and the phases beside it are work bzo does; outside is everything between one frame callback and the next, which is the browser, the GPU and the wait for the display. fastest is the shortest frame of the window and bounds the refresh interval from below — a frame rate on its own cannot tell a slow client from a 30Hz panel.

A session reports one of these every twenty seconds for as long as it lasts, marked reason=xrSession, so a run is a series rather than a single line taken wherever the player happened to be standing. The mode that produced them is on the renderer.init.ok line above the first one.
